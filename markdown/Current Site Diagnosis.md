# Ama's Plant Corner Site Diagnosis
| Symptom | Root cause | Who is affected | What it matters to the business |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product images do not show up. | The three image elements request `images/houseplants.jpg`, `images/pots.jpg`, and `images/soil.jpg`, but no `images` directory or image files are present in the workspace. The browser therefore has no supplied files at those paths. | Visitors trying to judge the appearance, variety, or condition of plants and products before deciding whether to visit or ask about them. | Missing product imagery weakens product discovery and trust, and may reduce visits and sales. |
| Image alternatives say only “image.” | Every product image has the same generic `alt="image"` text instead of a description of its subject. | Screen-reader users, people browsing with images disabled, and visitors on unreliable or slow connections who rely on alternative text. | These visitors lose useful product context, making the catalog less accessible and less informative; this can reduce confidence and exclude potential customers. |
| Sections after the product images stay in a narrow left-hand column. | The later sections (About, Plant Care Tips, and Ask Ama) are all inside `#main`, which is floated left at a fixed width of 610px. They therefore inherit that column rather than receiving their own full-width layout. | Visitors who expect the shop information and care guides to use the page width, especially those reading on larger screens or scanning for key details. | The page feels unbalanced and wastes available space, making important information harder to find and the site appear unfinished. |
| “Get In Touch” sits isolated at the far left. | `#contact` is outside the centered `#content` container, and the stylesheet has no layout or width rules for `#contact`. It falls back to normal document flow at the left edge. | Visitors trying to contact Ama, particularly people who have scrolled through the page and expect the contact form to be clearly grouped and easy to locate. | A disconnected form is easier to overlook and can reduce completed inquiries and the site’s credibility. |
| The page is not responsive on mobile; content is scaled down or squeezed. | There is no viewport meta tag or responsive media query. The header, banner, content, and footer use fixed 900px widths; the main and sidebar use fixed widths and floats. | Mobile visitors, including customers browsing while away from home, who must zoom, pan horizontally, or struggle with small text and controls. | Poor mobile usability can drive visitors away before they find products, directions, or contact details, costing potential sales and inquiries. |
| Plant-care topics and facts are not visually distinguished. | The stylesheet contains no rules for `.care-guides`, `.guide-trigger`, or `.guide-body`; topic labels are ordinary `div` elements and the facts are ordinary paragraphs. | Customers trying to quickly scan for advice relevant to a particular plant, especially novice plant owners looking for practical instructions. | Hard-to-scan advice is less useful, weakens the site’s helpfulness, and may reduce customer confidence in the shop’s expertise. |
| Contact fields provide weak input guidance and validation. | The fields are plain text inputs, including the email and phone fields; there are no associated labels, and placeholders are the only field descriptions. | Visitors using assistive technology, people who need to identify fields after entering text, and customers who mistype contact details. | Missing or invalid contact details can make inquiries difficult to answer; inaccessible or confusing fields can lower form completion. |
| Section headings skip from the page title to level-four headings. | The page uses an `<h1>` for the banner title, then `<h4>` for section headings, with no `<h2>` or `<h3>` hierarchy. | Screen-reader users navigating by headings and visitors scanning the page structure; search engines also receive a less coherent outline. | The content is harder to navigate and understand, reducing accessibility and potentially weakening search presentation. |
